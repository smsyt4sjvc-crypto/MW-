# Project Jupiter: credit, construction and power map

**As of:** September 21, 2026 · **Location:** Doña Ana County, New Mexico · **Status:** construction underway; onsite-generation permitting and loan syndication stressed

## Bottom line

Project Jupiter is a live test of the AI-infrastructure financing loop. Oracle is the disclosed tenant/economic anchor for a large campus developed through separate project companies. Approximately $18B of project financing was arranged around that structure. Financial Times reporting placed private indications for the loans at 89–91 cents on the dollar on September 18 after broad investor syndication stalled.

That mark matters because it suggests the external capital channel is demanding a material discount for tenant-credit and delivery risk. It does **not** establish a payment default, acceleration, project cancellation, recovery value or an executed arm's-length trade. It also does not establish that the full $18B is direct Oracle corporate borrowing.

## Entity and cash-flow map

| Role | Disclosed party or vehicle | What is known |
|---|---|---|
| Tenant / economic anchor | Oracle America Cloud Services LLC, formerly Green Chile Ventures | Campus tenant; detailed lease commencement, acceptance, guarantee and recourse terms are not public in the sources read. |
| Data-center developer/operator | STACK Infrastructure | STACK-affiliated project structure; Blue Owl Capital is associated with the equity backing. Exact first-loss waterfall is unavailable. |
| Power co-developer | BorderPlex Digital Assets | Co-developer for onsite power and related infrastructure. |
| Microgrid company | Yucca Growth Infrastructure LLC | Applicant for the onsite microgrid and fuel-cell air permit. |
| Data-center companies | Red Chiles A, B, C and D LLC | Project companies for four data-center buildings and equipment. |
| Generation supplier | Bloom Energy | Proposed solid-oxide fuel-cell platform. |
| Debt syndicate | Approximately 20 banks | SMBC, BNP Paribas, Goldman Sachs and MUFG were reported as leading/administrative banks. |

## Physical-delivery facts

| Measure | Latest sourced figure | Boundary warning |
|---|---:|---|
| Estimated investment through June 30, 2026 | $4.7B | Project-company estimate; do not confuse with the similarly sized benefits estimate. |
| Data-center construction completion | ~9% | Does not imply accepted or energized IT MW. |
| Microgrid construction completion | ~10% | Early works and permitting remain material dependencies. |
| Proposed onsite generation | 2,462MW | Generation capacity, not facility load, IT load or effective utilized IT MW. |
| Loan / proposed generation MW | ~$7.31M | Debt/capacity intensity only; not capex per IT MW or revenue per MW. |
| Reported schedule slip | At least seven months | Baseline and revised milestone schedule are not publicly reconciled. |

Oracle announced a change from an earlier gas-turbine concept to Bloom fuel cells. The permit application seeks continuous operation of 2,462MW of generating capacity. Oracle has argued that the data-center buildings are distinct from the microgrid and do not themselves require the air permit; that distinction means a permitting dispute can delay power delivery without proving that all building work has stopped.

## Oracle balance-sheet context

At August 31, 2026, Oracle reported:

- $125.337B of current and noncurrent notes payable and other borrowings.
- $43.806B of recognized operating and finance lease liabilities.
- $288B of additional, substantially all data-center lease commitments not yet commenced, expected to begin from FY2027 Q2 through FY2029 with 15–19-year terms.
- $34.15B of unconditional purchase and other obligations, primarily cloud-infrastructure components and data-center power.
- $664B of remaining performance obligations.
- $11.363B Q1 change in deferred revenues from customer prepayments with a significant financing component.
- $19.909B of Q1 FY2027 net proceeds from at-the-market common-stock issuance.
- $28.499B of Q1 FY2027 capital expenditure and negative $5.396B of operating cash flow less capex.

These are economically different categories. The uncommenced leases are not on-balance-sheet debt at the reporting date; RPO is backlog rather than cash or recognized revenue; the prepayment figure is a quarterly cash-flow change in deferred revenue rather than a balance; equity issuance is financing rather than operating revenue.

## Financing loop

1. Oracle's long-term tenancy supports the project companies' ability to borrow.
2. Construction, permitting and tenant-credit concerns weaken investor demand for that project debt.
3. A stalled syndication leaves more exposure with arranging banks and raises the price demanded by marginal lenders.
4. More expensive or less available project capital can slow future capacity delivery, pushing Oracle revenue recognition and customer acceptance to the right.

This loop is a supported risk mechanism, not proof that Project Jupiter will fail. The strongest disconfirming evidence is that approximately $4.7B had already been invested by June 30, the four data-center buildings were reported under construction, Oracle had raised substantial equity capital, and its RPO remained very large.

## What would resolve the open questions

- Executed credit agreement: exact borrowers, funded amount, draw schedule, collateral, covenants, guarantees, recourse and syndication allocations.
- Executed Oracle lease: commencement, capacity-acceptance, delay, termination and payment conditions.
- Blue Owl/STACK equity documents: first-loss position, return hurdles and capital-call obligations.
- Updated permits and construction schedules: critical path from fuel-cell installation through energized and accepted capacity.
- IT design evidence: accepted IT-load MW, redundancy/reserve, PUE and period-average utilization.

Until those arrive, Project Jupiter cannot support a defensible capex-per-IT-MW, revenue-per-effective-MW or cash-payback calculation.
